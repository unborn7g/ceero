A calmer dog store, built to survive its own ad test

Brand, product range, unit economics, a Medusa + Next.js store, CJ fulfilment, Paystack checkout, and a 10-week launch with a loss limit you set before you spend. Track A (Nigeria) launches first; the build is structured so Track B (US/EU) is a configuration change, not a rebuild.

1. Bottom line

Build a calm-home dog brand called CeeRO on a Medusa + Next.js store, fulfil through CJ Dropshipping with a local fallback, take payments with Paystack, and launch a paid test in ten weeks. Budget ₦350,000 for a lean start — samples, registration, hosting and the first ₦150,000 of ads — up to ₦1,000,000 if you add held stock and the second ad tranche. That is roughly $230–650 at ₦1,550/$, including registration and excluding your own time. The earlier $800–1,600 figures reflect Track B (US/EU) assumptions; if you open it later, budget at that level.

Why a brand and not a generic store. Reported net margins land around 15–20% after ads, fees and refunds, and only about a tenth of stores turn a profit in year one. The survivors are built around a brand and repeat buyers, not one trending product. This plan ranks brand, contribution margin and repeat purchase above speed — and puts a number on each.

Already done

A working storefront demo exists in this workspace: ceero-store/index.html. Seven products, product pages with variants, cart drawer, discount codes, currency toggle (₦/$), and a full demo checkout. Use it to pressure-test the flow before a single naira of ad spend, and as the visual spec a developer builds against.

2. Decisions locked

These were the three open questions. They are now answered, and everything downstream assumes them.

Where your customers are — Track A first, Track B ready. Launch in Nigeria, in naira, with Paystack. Keep the catalogue, content and storefront processor-agnostic: prices live in a single table, currency is a display layer, and the checkout adapter is swappable so adding Stripe later is a two-day job, not a rebuild.
Custom build — yes, but not from scratch. Medusa (open-source commerce backend) + Next.js storefront. You own the data and pay no platform percentage, at the cost of owning uptime and integrations. Card entry stays on Paystack's hosted page, so you never touch card data or carry that compliance burden.
Loss limit — ₦250,000. Not real money yet: nothing is spent until the store and suppliers pass the launch gates. The number is set now so it cannot drift later. Full kill criteria in §12.
Brand — CeeRO. Short, ownable, easy to say in Lagos and in a US ad. Positioning, palette and voice in §5.

Validation gate: before a supplier order, all five must be true

  • 10 comparable products on Amazon Best Sellers with 500+ reviews (proof of demand, not proof of margin)
  • Google Trends flat or rising over 12 months for the product term
  • Supplier cost under 35% of your selling price, landed — not FOB
  • You have held a sample. It survives a wash, or a drop, or a dog
  • You can describe who buys it in one sentence without saying "dog owners"

Anything failing two or more is dropped, not renegotiated. Seven products is the ceiling for launch; the range will grow from repeat-purchase data, not from supplier catalogues.

Ship from inside the buyer's region

Direct China-to-customer is the model the new US rules hit hardest. For Track A, that means a Lagos warehouse or a local wholesaler holding stock; for Track B, regional 3PL stock. Freight-forwarding a batch is a different business from dropshipping, and a better one at this price point.

What "Track B ready" means concretely

  • One prices table keyed by currency in Medusa — no price strings in templates
  • Checkout adapter interface: paystack today, stripe later, same order-creation webhook contract
  • Shipping zones and tax rules per region, configured — not hard-coded to Nigeria
  • English copy that does not depend on naira idioms; metric and imperial sizes both stored

5. The brand: CeeRO

CeeRO sells quiet, washable things for dogs living in small homes. Not a pet megastore — a short, opinionated range that a founder can answer questions about.

Positioning line

For dogs who pace, gulp and startle — seven well-made things that turn 8pm into a quiet house.

This is deliberately narrow. It excludes puppies-as-gifts, large-breed outdoor gear and anything medical. A narrow promise is easier to shoot, easier to write ad hooks for, and easier for a customer to repeat to a friend.

Voice

Calm, specific, slightly dry. Short sentences. Numbers instead of adjectives ("10 cm rim", not "ultra-plush"). No exclamation marks, no "fur baby" language, no claims about anxiety as a medical condition — describe behaviour you can see ("circles before lying down"), not diagnoses.

Before: "Treat your fur baby to the ultimate calming experience!" After: "A rim high enough to press a chin against. Cover washes cold."

Five brand promises (make these real or delete them)

  1. One job per product. No 6-in-1 gadgets.
  2. Everything washable or wipeable. Dog things live on the floor.
  3. Sized for small homes. Flats and shared rooms, not just gardens.
  4. 30-day calm guarantee. If the dog ignores it, refund — return shipping on us in Lagos and Abuja.
  5. We answer. WhatsApp replies within business hours, by a human who has held the product.
Naming note

Check availability before you print anything: ceero.com, ceero.ng, Instagram, TikTok, X, and a trademark search in Nigeria (and USPTO if you plan Track B). If the handles are taken by an unrelated brand, choose a two-syllable variant now rather than after packaging is ordered.

A store does not fail at checkout; it fails because the contribution on each order is smaller than the cost of getting that order. Decide the numbers first, then let them decide the ads.

Why the cheap products cannot carry the ads alone

A ₦22,500 lick mat contributes roughly ₦14,100 before ads. At a realistic ₦13,000 CAC it clears ₦1,100 — and the ₦19,500 gloves lose money on their own — and most single-item buyers of a cheap product do not come back. The bed and the bundle carry the store; the mat, gloves and bowl exist to raise average order value and to give you content that converts strangers. This is why the range is designed as a set, and why the bundle price is prominent on every product page.

The three levers, in order of power

1. AOV. Every ₦10,000 added to average order value is pure contribution if it comes from an existing customer. Bundles, free-delivery thresholds and post-add upsells beat price cuts.
2. CAC. Creative volume and honest product pages lower it; audience micro-targeting rarely does.
3. Cost of goods. Moves last. Batch importing instead of per-order CJ fulfilment typically cuts landed cost 20–35% — but only once volume is real.

Every figure above is an assumption with a source in the workbook. Open ceero-unit-economics.xlsx, change the yellow cells, and the break-evens recalculate.

Build sequence (fits inside weeks 3–5 of the timeline)

  1. Port the demo's catalogue and components into Next.js; keep the CSS system, replace state with Medusa SDK calls.
  2. Medusa regions and prices: NG (NGN) live; US/EU (USD/EUR) present but disabled.
  3. Checkout adapter: initialise Paystack transaction server-side, verify signature on webhook, create the order, send email. Idempotency key on the reference — this is the bug that double-charges people.
  4. Fulfilment hook: on order paid, create CJ order or enqueue into the daily manual list, write the CJ order ID back to the order.
  5. Shipping rules: flat fee under threshold, free over ₦50,000, state-based ETA copy.
  6. Instrumentation: pixel events on add-to-cart, begin-checkout, purchase; server-side purchase event with the same event ID as the browser one.
  7. Hardening: 404s, empty states, mobile at 360px, keyboard focus, page weight under 500 kB on first load, legal pages.
Custom build vs Shopify — say the trade out loud

A custom store adds roughly 4–6 weeks over a Shopify launch and makes you responsible for order sync, tax logic and uptime. In exchange you keep the data, pay no platform percentage, and own a design competitors cannot buy for $39/month. If you would rather test demand in two weeks than build for six, launch the ad test against the demo store with a payment link and build in parallel — the plan supports both, but not silently.

Definition of "store ready to take money"

  • Paystack live keys, a real test transaction, and a refund done end to end
  • Webhook verified on a server that can be reached; retries handled
  • Order → supplier handoff tested with one real order to your own address
  • Every product page has: dimensions, materials, care, delivery ETA, return terms
  • Legal: terms, privacy (NDPA-aligned), returns policy, contact details, business name
  • Analytics verified: a purchase shows once in Meta, once in GA4, not twice
  • Load test at 100 concurrent users without 500s (a single Instagram post can do this)

8. Fulfilment ops

The order handoff is where most dropshipping stores lose their reputation: the customer paid you in ninety seconds, and now waits eleven days with no idea what is happening. Every rule below exists to shorten that silence.

The route for each SKU

Beds (bulky, hero)Batch import 20–30 units, hold in LagosCJ per-order if stock runs out2–4 days Lagos/Abuja, 3–6 nationwide
Lick mats, gloves, snuffle matsCJ per-order (light, cheap to ship)Local importer3–7 days
Ceramic bowls (fragile)Local stock, double-boxedCJ with protective packing2–5 days

Rule: never promise a date you do not control. The ETA on the product page is the maximum you genuinely expect, and when a delay happens you email before the customer notices. A delay admitted early reads as competence; a delay discovered late reads as a scam.

Operating rhythm

WhenWhatTime
Daily, 9amExport yesterday's paid orders; place with CJ / pick from local stock; message every buyer with a dispatch confirmation30–45 min

Returns and the guarantee

  • 30 days, unused, original packaging. Refund to the original payment method within 3 business days of the item arriving back.
  • Lagos and Abuja: arrange pickup with your courier (budget ₦3,000–5,000 per return). Elsewhere: customer drops at a partner courier, you refund the return cost as store credit.
  • Damaged in transit — replace immediately, no return required, photo only. Claim from the supplier separately; do not make the customer the investigator.
  • Track a reason code on every return: too small / dog ignored it / damaged / late / not as described. The codes, not the refund rate, tell you what to fix.
  • Reserve 3% of revenue for refunds and 1% for chargebacks in the model. Under-reserving is how a profitable month becomes a cash problem.
SOP card — keep this visible

Order paid → message the buyer within 2 hours with an ETA and your WhatsApp number → dispatch → send tracking same day the supplier books it → day 3 → if no tracking movement, apologise and give a firm new date → day 7 → ask how the dog is getting on, ask for a review, offer the next purchase code.

9. Payments & money

Paystack setup, in order

  1. Register a business account (CAC registration first — see §10); personal accounts create settlement problems later.
  2. Complete compliance: business documents, bank account in the business name, website with working terms and contact details. A store with no policy pages gets rejected.
  3. Test mode first: run a ₦1,000 transaction, refund it, confirm the webhook fires and the order appears in Medusa.
  4. Go live. Turn on the hosted checkout — never build a card form yourself.
  5. Enable bank transfer and USSD alongside cards; in Nigeria they convert buyers who do not trust card entry.
Fee itemRateOn a ₦80,000 order

Rates checked against Paystack's published pricing, September 2026. Verify before you set prices — processors change cards, and the cap is per transaction, not per day.

Pay-on-delivery

Offer it in Lagos and Abuja only, on orders under a threshold, after you have a repeat customer base. POD has the highest conversion and the worst cash conversion: a rider holds your stock for a day, and refusal rates of 10–20% are normal. If you offer it, the rider takes a photo of the package and logs the outcome; unreconciled POD cash is how small stores quietly lose money.

Paying suppliers in dollars

  • CJ bills in USD. You collect in naira. That gap is an FX exposure, not an accounting detail.
  • Price with an FX buffer (the model assumes ₦1,550/$; re-price when the rate moves more than 5%).
  • Keep a dedicated domiciliary account or a card that reliably pays suppliers; failed supplier payments delay orders by days.
  • Batch supplier payments weekly — fewer, larger conversions are cheaper and easier to reconcile.

Bookkeeping minimum

  • One business bank account. No personal spending from it.
  • A ledger (Wave, Zoho Books, or a disciplined sheet) with these lines per month: revenue, COGS, shipping, fees, ad spend, refunds, software, other.
  • Weekly: reconcile Paystack settlements to orders. Monthly: gross margin, contribution after ads, cash on hand, and stock value.
  • Keep every receipt from day one — product cost, freight, packaging, ads. They are deductions, not clutter.

11. The 10-week plan

Ten weeks, one founder, roughly 12–18 hours a week. The store demo already exists, so the build weeks are port-and-integrate, not design-from-zero.

Week 1 — Validate, register, sample

Run the product gate on ten candidates. Register the business and bank account. Order samples of the top five SKUs. Open CJ and request quotes to Nigeria for each.

  • Output: five products passed, samples in transit, CAC submitted
Week 2 — Suppliers, pricing, brand files

Compare landed cost across CJ and local importers. Set prices from the workbook, not from competitors. Lock the palette and wordmark; order packaging and stickers.

  • Gate: every SKU at ≤35% landed-cost ratio, or removed
Week 3 — Build: catalogue and storefront

Medusa running with regions, products, variants and stock. Next.js storefront ported from the demo. Product copy written for all seven SKUs.

Week 4 — Build: checkout and orders

Paystack integration, webhook, order emails, shipping rules, legal pages. A real test order placed and fulfilled to your own address.

  • Gate: one end-to-end order with tracking, no manual fixes
Week 5 — Content and QA

Photograph the samples yourself (daylight, linen, real rooms). Cut six short videos from supplier footage plus your own tests. Full QA on mobile at 360px, slow connection, three browsers.

Week 6 — Soft launch to 20 people

Send the store to twenty real dog owners for free — in exchange for honest feedback and a filmed reaction. Watch five of them buy without help. Fix what confuses them.

  • Gate: 3 of 5 unassisted users complete a purchase
Week 7 — Ad accounts and tracking

Meta Business Manager, pixel, CAPI, catalogue. TikTok pixel. Two campaigns drafted, eight creatives ready. Set the ₦250,000 cap as a hard spend limit in the account, not in your head.

Week 8 — Live test, days 1–7

Launch on two broad audiences, no interest stacking. ₦8,000–10,000/day. Do not touch the campaign for 72 hours. Read the numbers against the gates in §12, not against your feelings.

Week 9 — Read, iterate, days 8–14

Kill losing creatives, double the winners' budget. Rewrite the product page for the SKU closest to break-even. Ask every buyer two questions by WhatsApp.

Week 10 — Decide

Scale / iterate / stop, against the written criteria. Whichever it is, write down what you learned and what you will do differently — that document is worth more than the ad spend.

The one rule that makes this plan work

No ads until weeks 1–7 are complete. Most failed stores spend the budget learning things a sample, a test order and five honest friends would have told them for free.

12. Ad test & loss limit

The point of the first ₦250,000 is not sales. It is to buy four answers: does the creative stop the scroll, does the product page convert, what does an order actually cost, and does anyone come back.

The funnel you are aiming at

StageAssumptionOn ₦10,000/day
ImpressionsCPM ₦2,5004,000/day
Clicks to site1.5% CTR60/day at ₦167 per click

These are planning assumptions, not benchmarks you should trust. Nigerian CPMs of $1–2 and pet-category conversion near 1.5% are the starting point; your own first week replaces them.

What happens if the site converts worse than that

The conversion rate is the assumption that decides whether this business works, and it is the one you control most directly through the product page. The same ₦140,000 of spend produces very different outcomes:

Session → order rateOrdersCPAContribution per orderVerdict
0.3%2.5₦55,600₦49,760Fails — above break-even

The break-even point sits near 0.36% on the bed. That single number explains the whole strategy: put the ad budget behind the highest-contribution product, make its page persuasive, and do not spend money sending cold traffic to the ₦19,500 gloves.

Test structure

  • Two campaigns: broad (age 25–55, Lagos/Abuja/Port Harcourt) and a lookalike from site visitors once you have 100 of them.
  • Four creatives per campaign: dog-in-bed problem/solution, the frozen mat routine, the "3 things to buy a nervous dog" list, and a founder-to-camera 20-second explainer.
  • Landing pages: bed and bundle pages for the first week. Do not split traffic across seven products with a ₦250,000 budget.
  • Optimise for purchase, not add-to-cart. Optimising for cheap intent trains the algorithm to find browsers.
  • Retargeting at 15% of budget from day 4: everyone who viewed a product and did not buy.

Loss limit — ₦250,000, in two tranches

Tranche 1: ₦150,000 over 14 days. If at least two of three gates pass (CPA ≤ break-even, CVR ≥ 1.2%, refund rate < 8%), release tranche 2: ₦100,000 for days 15–30. If fewer than two pass, stop and keep the remaining ₦100,000. Set it as a spending limit inside Meta and TikTok so the decision is enforced by the platform, not by willpower at midnight.

Cadence: three posts a week on Instagram and TikTok for eight weeks. Not because reach comes free, but because each post is a free ad test — the video that stops your own followers is the one to put money behind.

14. Repeat purchase

Reported dropshipping margins only work when a customer comes back. One order at break-even plus a second order at full contribution is a business; one order at break-even, forever, is a treadmill.

Tooling: Resend for transactional, a lightweight ESP (MailerLite free tier) for flows until volume justifies better. WhatsApp opt-in is asked for on the thank-you page, never pre-ticked.

Kill criteria — stop, do not "optimise"

  • After ₦150,000 of spend: fewer than 10 orders, or CPA more than 2× break-even across all creatives.
  • Refund rate above 15% on any SKU after 20 orders — the product is wrong, not the copy.
  • Two consecutive months of negative contribution after ads with no single identified fix.
  • You cannot fulfil within your advertised window for three consecutive weeks.

What "stop" means: pause ads, keep the store and the brand, fulfil outstanding orders, and either move to a local-first model (stock held, cheaper delivery, no ad-funded acquisition) or sell the range through Instagram and WhatsApp only. Stopping the ad test is not stopping the business.

18. Checklists

Launch day

  • A real card purchase from a friend's phone, refunded the same day
  • One order placed from your own account, fulfilled to your door with tracking
  • All seven product pages opened on a phone, on mobile data, not wifi
  • WhatsApp number live and answering; auto-reply set
  • Pixel firing: view, add-to-cart, begin-checkout, purchase — once each
  • Spending limits set on Meta and TikTok before the first campaign is switched on
  • Refund policy, privacy page, terms and business address reachable from the footer
  • Two people who will tell you the truth have looked at it

Weekly operating sheet (the six numbers)

  • Sessions, orders, conversion rate
  • Revenue, AOV, contribution after ads
  • Ad spend, CPA, best and worst creative
  • Refunds and their reason codes
  • Orders older than 48 hours without tracking
  • Cash on hand, stock on hand, next restock date

Track B trigger — do not start early

  • 90 consecutive days of positive contribution after ads in Nigeria
  • Repeat rate ≥ 15% and AOV ≥ ₦70,000
  • ₦2m+ set aside specifically for the expansion, not borrowed from operations
  • A confirmed regional fulfilment route with a tested 3–7 day delivery
